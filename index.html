New in 2026

Told your DWI could never come off your record? That answer just changed.

In 2026 the New Mexico Court of Appeals ruled that a first-offense DWI resolved by a successfully completed deferred sentence — and dismissed — may be eligible for expungement under the non-conviction track. Most websites, and plenty of lawyers, are still giving the old answer. If this is your situation, it's worth two minutes to check.

Two Paths Under the Act

How your case ended decides your track.

Non-Conviction · § 29-3A-4

Dismissals, acquittals & discharges

  • Court shall grant if requirements are met
  • 1-year wait from final disposition
  • Hearing only if an agency objects
  • Includes diversion, conditional discharge & traffic-code deferred sentences
Conviction · § 29-3A-5

Guilty pleas & verdicts

  • Judge must find justice will be served
  • 2–10 year wait from sentence completion, by offense level
  • Hearing in every case
  • Some offenses excluded — the quiz screens for them
